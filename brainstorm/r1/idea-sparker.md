idea-sparker. ROUND 1 (divergent, independent). TL;DR: 15 ideas, 5 upgrades, 5 ignore-the-plan, 5 wild. Best bets: column scan, frame crossfade, silence map.
Format per line: idea. Wow moment. Blind use (short).

1. ★ [UPGRADE: none] Column scan: the playhead sweeps the frame left to right like a barcode reader; each column becomes one chord (colour sets pitch class, brightness sets volume). Wow: the judge watches a bar cross the picture and hears the picture as a chord run. Blind: arrow keys step one column at a time, and each step is also spoken as a value.
2. [UPGRADE] Replace the Side A / Side B split with one "truth dial": one track morphs from musical to strict in a single turn of the dial. Wow: judges hear the same melody dissolve into raw data steps. Blind: slider with spoken position, plus +/- buttons for small steps.
3. [CUT] Cut the five-voice audio description from the plan, because five voices multiply build and test work for one judged moment; one narrator with a "describe this frame" button gives the same value. Wow: a cleaner, single-voice demo that is easier to follow. Blind: one voice means fewer overlaps with TalkBack speech.
4. Two-layer mixing desk: the user picks two layers (for example temperature and sea ice) and hears them panned left and right, with a shared rhythm. Wow: agreement sounds like harmony and disagreement sounds like clash. Blind: each layer has its own spoken name, pan position and on/off button.
5. ★ Frame crossfade: two frames from different years play as one sound that slides from the first to the second, so the change is heard as a glide, not counted. Wow: judges hear a long change as one smooth slide. Blind: a single "year A to year B" control with spoken year labels.
6. [RADICAL] Earth Radio: a 24/7 sound-only station that plays EIC-derived soundscapes with spoken station breaks, no app and no screen. Wow: a judge tunes it in a car and hears the planet come on between songs. Blind: works on any audio player; the weakest point is that it has no interaction, so it must not replace the jukebox.
7. Sound postcards for classrooms: a paper QR code opens a 30-second soundscape with a spoken caption, and it works offline after one download. Wow: a teacher hands out paper and a blind student hears the Indian Ocean warm up. Blind: QR scan through a screen reader [assumption: TalkBack and VoiceOver can read a QR code].
8. Braille sheet: a printable tactile graphic of a simplified frame, with the sound as the second layer. Wow: a student feels the raised shape of the warm patch with their fingers, then presses play. Blind: built for braille readers first; needs an embosser, so it is a stretch goal.
9. Feature-phone line: a phone number takes a spoken question ("where is it warmest?") and answers with a short sonic sting plus a spoken number. Wow: a judge calls from a basic phone and hears an answer with no smartphone. Blind: voice-only by design; the weakness is telephony cost and setup, which is not doable in 48 h.
10. ★ Silence map: missing data is played as rest, so the piece goes quiet where the record is thin, and the Narrator says why. Wow: judges hear a hole in the music and realise "no data" is not "zero". Blind: the silence is announced in speech and in text. Bet because it makes the honesty rule the centrepiece.
11. Pulse duet: the user taps their own pulse (tap tempo), and the planet's frame plays against that tempo. Wow: the judge's heartbeat sets the rhythm of Earth's data. Blind: a large tap button; no camera or sensor needed.
12. Orbit surf game: a 60-second game where a dot follows a satellite-style track over the frame, and the sound under the dot is the map. Wow: the judge steers a dot and hears the planet under them. Blind: arrow keys or voice steering only. [unverified: orbit track data source]
13. Sound poster: a printed EIC-style frame carries a hidden audio tone; hold a phone over it and the story plays. Wow: a poster on a wall plays the planet when a phone hovers over it. Blind: audio-only playback, so it works without sight. Weakness: encoding and print calibration are hard in 48 h.
14. Scenario dial: the user drags a slider from observed values to a published projection, and the sea becomes louder as they move. Wow: judges hear the gap between "what happened" and "what might happen". Blind: slider with spoken values. [unverified: projection source and licence] Weakness: a projection must be labelled so it never reads as data.
15. Class choir: each person records one spoken year, and the app sings the whole timeline as a choir of voices. Wow: a room of students sings a timeline and hears it resolve. Blind: voice input, and each singer hears their own note. Weakness: needs consent handling for recordings.

★ reasons:
- ★1 Column scan: it makes the frame the literal source of every note, which is what judges will look for first.
- ★5 Frame crossfade: change over time becomes something you hear, not a number you read, and it uses only two layers.
- ★10 Silence map: it turns the rule "missing data is silence" into the strongest moment, which is also the best answer to a Validity question.

Weakest point of my own proposal: several ideas (9, 13, 15) fail the 48 h test, and I have not checked which EIC frames are real images with usable colour scales. The mixing desk (4) could also imply correlation means cause, so it needs a clear label if we use it.
