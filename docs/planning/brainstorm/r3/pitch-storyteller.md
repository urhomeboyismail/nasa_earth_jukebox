# R3 Critique: pitch-storyteller

TL;DR: Options 1 and 4 can be told in 30 seconds and felt; Option 0 cannot. The best pitch is Option 1's blind-student test with Option 4's Mauritius ending.
Story rule: one hero (a blind student), one place, one "aha" in the first 10 seconds. Every extra feature costs story.

## Option 0: Baseline
- 10-year-old: "You say any picture of Earth and the app plays it as music."
- Hook: a judge shouts a topic; silence; then sound. Risky: if live mode fails, the hook is the failure.
- Fatal flaw (story): ten features, so no single memorable moment. Cannot be said in one breath. Live mode is a coin-flip on stage.
- Fix: cut to one story; keep one "judge picks a date" moment only on a prepared layer.
- Award: Best Use of Data (weak); honestly none.

## Option 1: One Story, Two Ears
- 10-year-old: "Same ice pictures, two kinds of sound, and a blind girl finds the melting by ear."
- Hook (5 s): a drone hums steadily; a student's hand lifts as the pitch steps away from it. Subtitle: "She cannot see this map. She just found the change."
- Fatal flaw: the pitch lives or dies on a tester that does not exist yet. Without a real number it is a claim. Also small scale: one story feels narrow for Influence.
- Fix: book a tester now; if none, use sighted blindfold users and label them honestly as such. Add a final "what the sound hides" slide for credibility. Use a Mauritius classroom as the closing image.
- Award: Best Storytelling (also strong for Most Inspirational).

## Option 2: Frame Jukebox
- 10-year-old: "A bar sweeps across a NASA picture and you hear the picture."
- Hook: visible playhead crosses a burning-red fire map, the sound swells where the fires are. Easy to see and hear at once.
- Fatal flaw: it explains the mechanism, not the human. Judges hear "pixel to pitch", which the brief calls noise. The story is a technique demo.
- Fix: put a person in every shot; open on the sweep but end on a blind user choosing a year. Keep the daily "today's fires" record as the "it is live" proof.
- Award: Best Use of Data (frames literally are the music, most direct fit to the brief).

## Option 3: Time Under Your Finger
- 10-year-old: "Pick any place on Earth and hear how it changed over 40 years."
- Hook: "Where do you live?" Student says a place; the app plays that place's years. Strong and personal.
- Fatal flaw: place x time is two ideas, and the "aim the phone like a torch" gesture is hard to show in a 30 s video. Sensor and screen-reader behaviour is unverified `[unverified]`.
- Fix: demo only the Place and Year sliders, and make "my place" the whole pitch. Torch aiming is a bonus moment, not the core.
- Award: Most Inspirational / Art & Technology.

## Option 4: Hold the Ocean to Your Ear
- 10-year-old: "Hold your phone like a seashell and hear the sea around Mauritius change."
- Hook: phone lifted to the ear on a Port Louis shore, waves, then the pitch drifts. Beautiful and local.
- Fatal flaw: the data is Amber (Earthdata login, sources partly verified). If SST is shaky the whole story is shaky. "Sea rises here" invites a claim we must not overstate; SST is not sea level.
- Fix: confirm the SST layer on day one; state exactly which variable is heard. Keep the sea-level line as spoken context only, with a citation, not as sonified data. Use the duet only if the two series are both real.
- Award: Local Impact (Mauritius) and Galactic Impact; also Most Inspirational.

## Option 5: Tune Your Own Earth
- 10-year-old: "Blind people pick what high and low mean, then hear NASA's Earth their way."
- Hook: weak. A settings screen is not a first 5 seconds.
- Fatal flaw: tool without a story. It is empowering, but the demo is a menu. Also "who chose the mapping" weakens Validity unless tested.
- Fix: do not make it a product; make it one scene inside Option 1 (two mappings, A/B, user picks). Not standalone.
- Award: Art & Technology (a stretch).

## Recommendation (pitch lens)
Pitch spine: Option 1 (proof) + Option 4 (place) + one Option 2 visual (the sweep). Seven slides: 1 tester result, 2 problem, 3 the sound, 4 Side A/B, 5 silence = no data, 6 what the sound hides, 7 Mauritius and credits plus AI disclosure. No NASA logos on any slide. AI-use disclosure goes on slide 7 and the project page.

## Emotion vs evidence
Emotion: the student's moment of discovery and the hand on the phone. Evidence: tester numbers, the NSIDC cross-check `[verified: https://nsidc.org/data/g02135]`, and the honest limits slide.

Weakest point of my own proposal: it leans on a blind-user result that does not exist yet and on my judgement that judges prefer one story; a broader Option 3 style pitch might score higher on Influence if the demo works, and I have not tested that.
